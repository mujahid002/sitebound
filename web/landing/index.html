Addressless checkout
for your website.

No one has to trust the page: who gets paid comes from the contract and the site the buyer's browser signs.

Only the domain's owner, proven by a DNS record, can choose the wallet its payments go to.

Every payment is bound to the site.

  1. The browser signs the site

    The buyer's browser reports which site they are on. The page cannot change it.

  2. The contract looks it up

    Only the wallet registered for that site counts. The page never supplies an address.

  3. The owner's wallet is paid

    USDT goes straight from the buyer. The contract holds nothing.

For website owners

  1. Type your domain

    The site you own, like example.com.

  2. Add one DNS TXT record

    Proves you control the domain, once.

  3. Paste a ~20-line snippet

    Your checkout opens the Sitebound frame. No address in your code.

For buyers

  1. First time

    Connect your wallet and create a passkey.

  2. After that: Touch ID

    Check the site and the amount, then confirm.

Check the contract yourself

  • Pays only the site the buyer is on.

    The payee is the wallet registered for the origin the buyer's browser signed, never an address from the page.

    Read the contract
  • The relayer cannot redirect a payment.

    It only submits what the buyer's passkey signed. Its attestor key registers a site's payout after the DNS check: a new site is live at once, and a change to an existing payout waits 1 hour, during which the current payout wallet can freeze it.

    See the tests
  • No admin key.

    No owner, no upgrade and no pause. The contract never holds funds: USDT moves straight from the buyer to the site's payout wallet.

Honest limits

  • Testnet only, not audited.

    Do not use it with money you cannot lose.

  • The relayer is a trusted step.

    Its attestor key registers a site's payout wallet after the DNS check. Only the owner of a domain can set where its payments go, because the DNS record names the wallet.

  • The site shown is what the browser reports.

    Sitebound does not vouch for what a site sells.
