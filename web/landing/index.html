The problem

Checkout code holds the payment address. A hacked or injected script swaps it.

The buyer copies the new address, pays, and the money is gone.

Even without an attack, copying 42 characters by hand goes wrong.

Not checkouts, same pattern: code in the page decided where the money went.

How it works

  1. Register once

    Type your domain, add one DNS record. Your site maps to your wallet on-chain.

  2. Buyer confirms

    Like 3-D Secure: the frame shows your site and the amount, and one passkey touch signs both. The browser writes the site, not the page.

  3. Contract pays

    pay() has no payee field. USDT goes to your site's registered wallet. Buyers pay only USDT, plus a 0.01 USDT fee; the relayer pays gas.

A hacked page cannot redirect the money.

Who it is for

Web2 businesses and creators. No wallet extension, no address to publish, no gas. One script tag and a button.

Example: 0xmujahid.xyz, a portfolio with a "Buy me a coffee" button.

Why Whitechain

The P-256 precompile lets the contract check every passkey signature on-chain. Its USDT supports permit, so buyers approve with a signature and never need gas.

Honest limits

  • One trusted attestor.

    Our relayer checks DNS and signs registrations alone.

  • Owners hold power.

    An owner can freeze payments or drop a partner's share at once.

  • No overlay detection.

    A hacked page can draw over the frame and lie. It cannot change who gets paid.
